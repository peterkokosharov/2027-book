## Executive Summary

This is not a novel about AI. It is a novel about **power** — who holds it, who pays for it, and who pretends it doesn't exist. The machines are the stage; the people are the play. And the play is brutal, funny, and uncomfortably precise.

## What Works

### 1. The Structure (Five Acts, Five Days, Five Years)
- **Act I: The Fire** (Apr 2022 – Mar 2023) — The spark: a leak, a demo, a valuation jump
- **Act II: The Five Days** (Mar – May 2023) — Compressed crisis; the "alignment" theater
- **Act III: The Arming of Nations** (May – Nov 2024) — Sovereign clusters, export controls, the hardware race
- **Act IV: The Tower** (Nov 2024 – Feb 2025) — The datacenter as ziggurat; power density as theology
- **Act V: The Meter** (Feb 2025 – late 2026) — Billing becomes governance; the ledger writes the law

Each act tightens the screw. The timeline *is* the plot.

### 2. The Houses (Not Factions — *Houses*)
Anvil, Prometheon, Meridian, Lyceum, Covenant, Agora, Helix, Lumen Diffusion.  
Each has a **founder**, a **metaphor**, and a **price**. They don't debate; they *bid*. The "Who is Who" section is the catalog of ships — and you need it.

### 3. The Towns (The Meter Reads Everyone)
Salt Fork water clerk. Nairobi night-shift labeler. Columbus backend engineer. Louisiana retired lineman. Sofia scaffold programmer. Boston fund manager.  
**The meter doesn't care about your title.** It reads your tokens. This is the democratic horror of the compute age.

### 4. The Glossary (Don't Skip It)
"Alignment" ≠ safety. "Alignment" = *the model does what the buyer paid for*.  
"Safety" = *the model doesn't embarrass the buyer in public*.  
"The Meter" = *the billing system that became the constitution*.  
These definitions reframe the entire book retroactively.

### 5. Prose Discipline
- No exposition dumps. You learn the world by watching people pay invoices.
- Scene breaks (`* * *`) are breath marks, not chapter padding.
- Numbers (prices, rounds, dates, GPU counts) are **real**. The small numbers in invented scenes are *scene-local*. The author tells you this upfront. Trust earned.

## The One Scene That Stays

> **The lineman in Ashby Parish (Louisiana) reads his water meter.**  
> The fund manager in Boston reads her GPU invoice.  
> The labeler in Nairobi reads her task queue.  
> *Same rhythm. Different currency.*

That parallelism — the meter as universal sacrament — is the book's thesis in three paragraphs.

## Final Thought

The Iliad ends with a funeral. This ends with a **billing cycle**.

> *"Where the record ends — late in 2026 — the Ledger goes on writing, and from that line onward its entries are forecasts: they describe no real person's acts. It says where it crosses."*

That line is the epitaph. And the invoice.
